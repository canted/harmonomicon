## Activity-level direction

Authors should be able to say “collect up to two text or audio contributions from each person,” then “for each accepted contribution, have someone read/play and share it,” or “vote on these contributions.” Accepted kinds and per-person cardinality are independent choices. Iterating every item, assigning one source and selecting an outcome remain different useful instructions. Historical text pooling, item loops and linked responses already reuse saved contributions.

## Exact bounded choices

- `pool@2` requires `id,op,prompt,kinds,perActor,visibility`; optional `round` defaults null and optional `input` defaults null. A supplied non-null round is a name unique among typed and routed rounds within the package; explicit null equals omission. Visibility is private/group; kinds is a distinct nonempty subset of text/image/audio; perActor is an exact integer 1–8 and is one shared upper limit across all accepted kinds, not a required minimum or a per-kind limit. Equal values/media refs under distinct accepted item IDs remain distinct contributions and candidates. Input can be a declared contribution binding or a preceding qualified selection (`select@2` or `select@3`), preserving 0.22 origin/handoff authority. Anonymous literals and copied labels are not inputs.
- Pool collection retains host-controlled effective actors and resolved opening/closing dates, inclusive opening/exclusive closing, trusted close/configure, immutable entries and exact retry handling. Filling quotas does not end the window. Quota counts all accepted entries by that actor in the invocation, including before withdrawal/readdition. Final source eligibility excludes withdrawn authors without deleting historical entries or ledger. Each pool has globally unique item IDs within its invocation. New windows require `host_controls@1`; media kinds require exact retained media capabilities.
- The compatible typed source family is `artifact_pool@1/@2/@3` and `pool@2`. New item/vote/assignment consumers accept this family and produce the same UUID-qualified material shape established in 0.22, with null round where absent. Old `pool@1` is an untyped-text contract with its own supported consumers; no implicit adapter or silently widened old version is introduced. New consumers handle all three language kinds; they do not need duplicate author settings to restate pool kinds.
- `for_items@2` has `id,op,source,policy,steps`; `policy:pool_order@1` freezes only final-eligible accepted items in commit order. Top-level only. Its body allows ordinary retained instructions that are already legal in an item body (including collect/reveal/tally/pause) plus the three new item instructions; no older top-level-only contract is widened; new pools/votes/assignments and all repetition remain top-level. Body outputs stay local. A private `state.typedItems[loopID]` stores the immutable items/final actor snapshot, with frame.itemLoop pointing to it; no loop wrapper record appears in public views. Current item context preserves source and stable item ID; private value/author metadata never leaks through public plan/context fields.
- `assign_item@2` keeps `id,op,policy,prompt,afterMs`, uses `policy:claim_reader@1`, and selects the first ordered claim from a final effective source actor other than the author. A participating organizer is eligible through that same source actor list, with no role bypass. Only the assigned reader receives the actual qualified contribution (including attribution) before publication; other viewers receive reader/eligibility metadata only. With no possible eligible reader it skips immediately with no reader. Timeout otherwise records no reader. `acknowledge@2` accepts only that reader's advance, skips without a reader, and records true/false; `reveal_item@2` publishes the actual qualified contribution only after true acknowledgment. Old anonymous author-concealment is not promised by these new attributed operations.
- Each ordinary body instruction still runs after a skipped reveal; the loop does not infer dependency or a conditional workflow. Examples must describe that path honestly. New reveal output is explicit in its own record, so publication need not reinterpret an old source pool's visibility contract. Media reads follow actual private/public projections, never reference-string possession.
- Qualified voting retains candidate freezing at voting entry, private/group ballots, optional current-vote changes, final-voter eligibility, private aggregates until presentation, exact most-votes/tie/no-vote/no-candidate behavior and durable policies. Each item, not each author, is a candidate. `select@3` supports the 0.22 explicit noVotes policies; retention is statically allowed only with an actual qualified starting input (new pool or artifact_pool@3), never an anonymous literal. Zero candidates always stays no_candidates. New pool inputs may consume either qualified selection version; old pool/result versions retain restrictions.
- `assign_artifacts@2` retains one saved non-self source, reuse allowed and unmatched skip. Recipients are unique final-effective actors in source order, or the contributing subset. New `policy:next_nonself_item@1` selects the next eligible author cyclically, then that author's earliest eligible accepted item; the old single-entry deterministic policy remains unchanged and is not reused for this extension. Seeded policy samples eligible items in commit order; actors with more items may have more exposure, with no balancing guarantee. `artifact_response@2` accepts one independent immutable response per effective matched actor, preserving the qualified assigned material; explicit reveal publishes accepted linked pairs only. Additional pool quota does not silently increase response quota.
- Exact new projections use the established qualified contribution shape. New pool views expose `round,input,blocked,count,perActor,ownCount,remaining,entries` plus effective eligibility/dates; visible entries are qualified snapshots. Typed item context is only `{instance,source,itemId}` in public frames; only the assigned reader sees `assignment:<qualified contribution>`, and a reveal record exposes `candidate:<qualified contribution or null>`. New source-assignment records save `{actor,candidate}` entries; the recipient sees its qualified `assignment`. Response payload is `{candidate:<qualified ref>,value}` and saves `{actor,source:<qualified contribution>,value}`; before reveal only its author sees the response and source, afterward linked pairs are public. Votes/outcomes retain the exact 0.22 qualified shapes.
- At most 100 effective actors, quota at most eight, therefore at most 800 finally eligible loop items. Retained 256 bound identities/32 sequence steps/64 IDs remain; historical withdrawn entries remain bounded by actor/quota rules. Conservative clock budgets account for the maximum typed loop expansion and all relative body timeouts, including host actors outside the original roster, when checking resolved dates and setup/control time fields. A null closing reserves no future clock interval. Near the safe-integer maximum, a late trusted close retains the existing capped-relative-deadline behavior: overdue item timeouts settle at MAX, without invented reader/publication or a new close-rejection rule.
- Host source lookup/read grants extend to the new actual contributions and qualified selections; whole-snapshot/all-viewer authority, immutable origin with separate via, and ownership boundaries from 0.22 remain mandatory. Retry/recovery never substitutes a source or redraws a result. No new cross-provider protocol, unlimited stream, video, role/calendar engine, moderation or product lifecycle is implied.

## Delivery and review

Implement the new format/schema and independent Python/JavaScript runtimes/SQLite validation hosts, examples, conformance, exact historical comparison, supported-profile/migration/decision accounting and evidence. Preserve reviewed 0.22 and all earlier files. Parent coordinates independent review of the implementation pin. Generic typed pooling is the smallest coherent author-level consolidation; versioned consumer extensions preserve old meanings despite additional token versions. No wholesale refactor or operation-count target is an acceptance gate.

A read-only design critique confirmed the consolidation and required explicit shared maximum/withdrawal accounting, final source-effective reader membership, private internal iterator snapshots, distinct IDs for equal values, and transparent multi-entry sampling exposure. These are settled above; the deterministic multi-entry policy receives a new token.
