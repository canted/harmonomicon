# Candidate 0.24 design checkpoint

October 2, 2026. Base: completed 0.23 `ba0530038747f5ed0ee5570d232e2ec72db64a40`. New isolated branch `codex/candidate-0-24`. Main, earlier candidates and playground are retained. No merge, publication, deployment or 1.0 decision.

`first_valid@1` is a top-level typed collection with `policy:rolling_pair@1`, configurable `windowMs` and `retryAfterMs`, accepted `kinds`, qualified contribution `input` or null, and explicit `queueInput` binding or null. It validates the finished contribution, commits one immutable entry, canonical qualified output, updated queue and closure together. First means authoritative acceptance order. Claims/reservations and later pool closure cannot substitute for this operation.

Package `queueInputs` declarations name required `invitation_queue` bindings. A trusted host resolves a closed first-valid record to its immutable qualified queue snapshot. Continuation must supply exactly the canonical predecessor named by that snapshot through existing contribution inputs. Queue source authority and canonical authority are checked separately. An initial invocation shuffles its participants once with persisted xorshift32 Fisher–Yates draws. A continuation consumes the saved order; no redraw.

- Invite the first two queue members other than the canonical author. Only those two may submit; all bound viewers see invitations and canonical material.
- Winner moves to back; loser is explicitly promoted to the front, followed by all other members in their saved relative order (including the formerly excluded author), then the winner. A later host-launched invocation receives the output queue and actual accepted contribution, with a fresh shared deadline. This deliberately resets the losing person's clock.
- At exclusive closing time, both invitees move to back, their attempts count toward an unsuccessful pass, and the next pair opens against unchanged canonical material. Open the replacement at the host's observed time, avoiding unseen retroactive invitations after a late worker tick.
- Decline moves that invitee back and records a failed opportunity. Replace them from the queue within the existing deadline, prioritizing people not yet attempted; the other invitee retains their offer. No third racing slot. Each invitee has a separate ticket. Replacements get fresh tickets; retained partners keep their ticket and deadline. Submissions also name the exact predecessor reference.
- Fewer than two eligible people closes with `paused`; one pass in which every eligible person has declined/expired closes with `exhausted`. The exhausted output distinguishes pass_complete from failure_budget. These export the unchanged canonical reference and queue, plus `notBefore = close time + retryAfterMs`. Host may launch a successor after that boundary; no automatic unlimited loop. At most 100 initial/current members, 256 lifetime bound identities and 100 failed expiry/decline/replacement transitions per invocation (expiry counts once even with two invitees); membership changes cannot extend a failed pass indefinitely.
- Trusted `roster` events replace active membership: retain surviving order, append newcomers, preserve prior author exclusion and failed-opportunity budget. Withdrawn tokens retain historical viewing identity. Refill current offers within the same deadline; pause if insufficient. New viewers need canonical all-viewer authority. The demo hosts deny a genuinely unbound origin viewer; preauthorized viewers may join/rejoin. No queue copy grants read authority. Stable two-person roster normally needs a third available member after a contribution. Odd tails pair the remaining unattempted person with the earliest attempted eligible partner. Failed identities survive withdrawal/readdition within a pass; successor retry starts a fresh pass.

The host supplies authenticated actors, serial transaction acceptance, immutable ready owner-controlled media, clock, persistence, notification delivery and successor launch. Queue snapshots are immutable and must be consumed at most once into a successor, with durable creation retry identity keyed to the actual qualified source record. Duplicate qualified refs under different binding aliases reject. Failed setup consumes nothing. Exact creation retries return the original successor even after its offers expire; changed retries reject. The package expresses selection, deadlines and transitions; hosts do not dispatch on activity names.

Schema and both semantic validators reject unsupported settings/versions, orphan queue declarations, inconsistent queue/canonical bindings and unsafe clocks before target state or grants. If a late observed clock cannot fit a full fresh window plus backoff, close clock_exhausted with reason clock_limit and notBefore:null. That queue is non-resumable; never clamp a cooldown. Preserve all historical operation semantics. Demonstrate independent Python/JavaScript engines, SQLite hosts, actual PNG authority, racing submissions, invalid attempts, stale windows/predecessors, boundary/replay/restart, shared 24-hour rollover, expiration/exclusion, small roster, bounded backoff, membership, cross-instance grants and queue consumption, retained compatibility, and a different data-only composition.

Honest limits: carry-over preserves opportunity rather than a guaranteed win. An inactive person can remain invited indefinitely while others respond quickly. Timing and delivery are host engineering; this candidate does not provide calendars, accounts, moderation, video, product lifecycle or an unlimited workflow engine.
