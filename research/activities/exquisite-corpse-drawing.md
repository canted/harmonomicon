# Exquisite Corpse, drawing

## Identity and context

- **Description:** People secretly draw successive sections of one image, then reveal the combined result.
- **Group and mode:** Friends, family, or artists; the cited National Gallery of Art activity uses paper passed between people. It does not set a group size, though its example divides paper into three sections.
- **Facilitator:** Someone explains the folds and passing order; participants carry out the turns.
- **Experience:** Surprise through constrained collaboration.

## Preparation

Fold a sheet of paper top to bottom into three sections. Provide drawing materials and agree who receives it first, second, and third.

## Procedure

1. The first person draws in the top section.
2. They fold their drawing out of view, leaving a few connecting lines visible over the fold, then pass the sheet to the next person.
3. The next person draws in the newly available section, connecting to the visible lines without seeing the hidden drawing. They fold and pass on.
4. Repeat until the sections are complete.
5. Unfold the sheet and reveal the composite drawing.

## Rules and choices

- **Turn order:** One contributor at a time, with a sheet passed to the next contributor.
- **Information:** Earlier drawings remain hidden; only short continuation marks cross the fold.
- **Completion:** All prepared sections have contributions, then the full sheet is revealed.
- **Host choice:** Number of sheets, passing order, and whether to use the source's three-part body prompt.

## Variants

The [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/stories/articles/exquisite-corpse-kerry-james-marshall-and-friends) describes an artist project in which assigned body sections were mailed separately and assembled later. That is a documented remote variation, but its coordination and concealment rules differ from the paper-passing activity and would need a separate detailed card for implementation.

## Exceptions and open questions

The activity instructions do not specify how to handle a missing contributor, an unfinished section, accidental viewing of the hidden part, or contributions that do not meet at the guide lines.

## Access and participation

The paper version requires visual or tactile access to marks and a way to contribute to a drawing. The source gives no adaptation rules. A digital version would need to specify which marks are visible to the next contributor and when the completed image becomes public; this is an implementation analysis, not a source rule.

## Mechanisms and possible software role

**Mechanisms:** Ordered assignment, concealed prior work with limited shared context, final reveal. Software can enforce a visibility window, record sections, and assemble the result. In the physical version, folds and handoff do this without software.
