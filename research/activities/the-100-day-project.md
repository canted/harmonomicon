# The 100 Day Project

## Identity and context

Participants choose a creative practice and return to it daily for 100 days. They work on separate projects in parallel. Public sharing is optional; the community offers prompts, encouragement, and ways to find one another's work.

## Documented procedure

1. Choose a project or repeatable action. It may produce 100 finished things or incrementally develop one work.
2. Spend some time on it each day for 100 days; even a few minutes counts.
3. Share the process through a social hashtag, a friend, a private record, or another venue if desired. The newsletter supplies updates and inspiration; paid subscribers are offered daily prompts and chat.

## Rules and gaps

- No official registration is required. A participant can start independently or with friends outside the official round.
- The project does not require a public submission every day or a common creative prompt. These are optional participation routes.
- The consulted source gives no formal winner, missed-day penalty, proof of work, or review allocation. A host should not infer these from the 100-day label.

## Mechanisms and software boundary

**Mechanisms:** self-selected long-running project, daily pacing, optional prompt, optional public/private sharing, cohort visibility, persistence across missed or quiet days. This tests whether a format can express a **soft practice invitation** rather than forcing every day into a required submission.
