# Freeze Scenes

## Identity and context

- **Description:** Small groups create and present still scenes based on a prompt.
- **Group and mode:** Arts On The Move specifies groups of five or six within a drama club for ages 7–12; in person and live.
- **Facilitator:** A teacher supplies scene titles, countdowns, and reflection.
- **Experience:** Shared dramatic expression using bodies, faces, position, and space.

## Preparation

Give each group space to form a still image. Choose a scene title such as the source's beach or classroom examples.

## Procedure

1. Split participants into groups of five or six and announce a scene title.
2. Give groups up to one minute to plan and make a still image together.
3. Count down from ten. Each group presents its freeze simultaneously.
4. Let groups see one another's freezes and comment on what seems effective.
5. Repeat with other scene titles as desired.

## Rules and choices

- **Roles:** Teacher prompts; group members jointly choose characters, placement, and expression.
- **Trigger:** The teacher's countdown ends preparation and starts the reveal.
- **Visibility:** Planning occurs in each small group; finished images are public.
- **Completion:** A prompted freeze and discussion completes a round. The lesson repeats for several titles.
- **Host discretion:** Scene titles, number of rounds, and feedback facilitation.

## Variants

The lesson adds **thought-tracking**: groups show a freeze one at a time and each character speaks a word or sentence about how they feel while holding position. This changes the reveal from simultaneous to sequential and adds character turns. [Source](https://www.artsonthemove.co.uk/e_shop/samplescripts/DramaClubStarterPack_sample.pdf)

## Exceptions and open questions

The source does not explain absent members, groups smaller than five, disagreement over a scene, or a participant declining to perform.

## Access and participation

The activity requires a way to join group planning and express a still scene; standing, particular body positions, and speech in the thought-tracking variant may not work for everyone. The source does not document adaptations. The host should discuss role and position choices with participants rather than infer one universal form.

## Mechanisms and possible software role

**Mechanisms:** Parallel subgroup work, countdown barrier, simultaneous reveal, optional sequential character speech. Software could present prompts and timers or store photos if separately agreed. It cannot verify an embodied freeze or the quality of a discussion on its own.
