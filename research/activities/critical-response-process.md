# Critical Response Process

## Identity and context

- **Description:** A facilitator guides a maker and responders through four ordered kinds of feedback on a work in progress.
- **Group and mode:** One maker, one or more responders, and a facilitator; live discussion after a work is presented. The creator's published summary does not set a group size or medium.
- **Facilitator:** Starts each step, keeps responses within its rules, and helps maintain a useful dialogue.
- **Experience:** Constructive information for the maker about their work.

## Preparation

The maker presents work in progress. Identify maker, responders, and facilitator. A responder may also take notes. Before the process, the facilitator may establish whether suggestions for changes are welcome.

## Procedure

1. **Statements of meaning:** Responders say what they found meaningful or striking. They hold doubts and negative opinions for later.
2. **Maker as questioner:** The maker asks about the work; responders answer on topic. Opinions are allowed only as direct answers and without suggestions for changes.
3. **Neutral questions:** Responders ask questions that do not hide an opinion; the maker answers.
4. **Permissioned opinions:** A responder states the subject of an opinion and asks whether the maker wants to hear it. The maker may say no. If permission is given, the responder shares the opinion.

## Rules and choices

- **Role permissions:** Who may ask, answer, or express an opinion changes by phase.
- **Phase trigger:** The facilitator moves the group from one step to the next; no fixed durations are specified.
- **Visibility:** Work and feedback are shared with the group. The source does not require public posting or a stored artifact.
- **Completion:** The four core steps finish; optional later steps may follow.
- **Consent:** The maker controls whether to hear each offered opinion in step four.

## Variants

The creator's [brief guide](https://lizlerman.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Critical-Response-Process-in-Brief_CRP-one-pager_updated-2020_03_24.pdf) describes optional later discussion of the maker's next step, subject matter, or direct experimentation. It also describes facilitator handling of proposed fixes. These are additions or constraints, not part of the four-step minimum.

## Exceptions and open questions

The source does not specify exactly how the facilitator selects responders, handles interruptions, records consent, or resolves a contribution that violates a step. Human judgment is central to neutral-question and on-topic distinctions.

## Access and participation

Participants need access to the work and a communication mode for dialogue. The source provides no specific accommodations. Permission to hear an opinion is documented; permission to participate at all or to pass on a question is not specified.

## Mechanisms and possible software role

**Mechanisms:** Ordered phases, role-specific speech rights, per-opinion permission gate, facilitator intervention. Software could show phase instructions and request/record permission in an online setting, but it cannot reliably determine whether a question is neutral or a response useful to the maker without human review.
