# 1-2-4-All

## Identity and context

- **Description:** A group answers a common challenge through individual, pair, quartet, and full-group stages.
- **Group and mode:** The source gives a minimum of four, in person or online; live and about 15 minutes.
- **Facilitator:** A host states the challenge, keeps time, and cues regrouping.
- **Experience:** Ideas are generated individually, then shared and refined in increasingly large groups.

## Preparation

Form a clear challenge or invitation. Arrange space or online breakout groups for pairs and quartets; writing materials are optional.

## Procedure

1. Host presents the challenge (about one minute).
2. Each participant reflects silently and may write ideas (one minute).
3. Participants form pairs, share and build on ideas (two minutes).
4. Pairs join into quartets and refine ideas (five minutes).
5. Return to the full group. A few participants share ideas the whole group should hear (seven minutes).

## Rules and choices

- **Group transitions:** Alone → pair → quartet → full group.
- **Timing:** The source assigns durations by stage; the host cues each transition.
- **Visibility:** Individual reflection begins privately; discussion widens at each stage.
- **Completion:** Whole-group sharing ends the round.
- **Host choice:** The challenge, exact pairings, and which ideas are surfaced; the source does not specify a universal selection algorithm.

## Variants

The [source](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-2-4-all) describes 1-3-All for online settings where merging pairs into quartets is difficult. It also mentions reversing the order or running another round. Those change the group trajectory and should be declared as variants, not silently treated as the same trace.

## Exceptions and open questions

Pairs of three are permitted when needed; the source does not fully specify group partitioning for every total size, late arrivals, early exits, or silent groups.

## Access and participation

The activity needs a way to receive the prompt and contribute during increasingly public conversation. Online use requires working breakout access. The source mentions optional writing and an online chat sharing alternative; it does not specify personal access accommodations or a pass rule.

## Mechanisms and possible software role

**Mechanisms:** Timed phase progression, changing group partitions, widening information visibility, human synthesis. Software could manage timers and breakout assignments; a host still selects the prompt and facilitates sharing.
