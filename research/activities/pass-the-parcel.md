# Pass the Parcel

## Identity and context

- **One-sentence description:** Participants pass a layered parcel around a circle while music plays; the holder removes one layer whenever the music stops.
- **Occasions/settings:** Children's birthday parties, street parties, early-years groups, and other gatherings.
- **Group:** A circle of children with a host. The cited street-party guide calls it suitable for ages 5–10; an early-years adaptation includes a practice round for younger children. Neither source establishes a universal age or group-size limit.
- **Mode:** In person and live.
- **Facilitator:** A person prepares the parcel, explains the rules, controls the music, and manages turns.
- **Purpose or experience:** Shared anticipation, a brief turn in the spotlight, and a final reveal. A discussion variant uses each opened layer to prompt a group conversation.

## Preparation

- **Materials or inputs:** One parcel wrapped in distinct layers, an item at its center, and music. Layers may contain small treats or actions.
- **Host setup:** Decide the number of layers, any layer contents, the final item, and whether to spread turns deliberately. Arrange participants in a circle and explain the passing direction.
- **Participant preparation:** None beyond understanding the passing and unwrapping rules. A practice run is documented for an early-years version.

## Procedure

1. The host gives the parcel to a participant and starts the music.
2. While music plays, participants pass the parcel around the circle in one direction.
3. The host stops the music. The current holder removes **one** layer and receives any treat or follows any instruction in that layer.
4. The host restarts the music. Repeat passing, stopping, and removing one layer.
5. The game ends when the last layer is removed and the center item is revealed.

This is the shared core described by the United Reformed Church holiday-club guide and the street-party guide. The early-years version also explicitly ends at the object's reveal. See sources below.

## Rules and choices

- **Roles and turn/assignment rule:** The current parcel holder gets the unwrapping turn when music stops. The host chooses when to stop it.
- **Timing and triggers:** Music playing permits passing; music stopping triggers one unwrapping turn. Sources do not prescribe a fixed duration between stops.
- **What participants may choose:** In the cited basic rules, participants do not choose the recipient or the layer. A layer may ask them to perform an action.
- **What is visible to whom, and when:** The parcel and current holder are visible. Contents underneath each remaining layer are concealed until unwrapped.
- **Completion condition:** No wrapped layers remain; the final item is revealed.
- **Host discretion or intervention:** The street-party guide says to try to avoid repeat unwrapping turns. The early-years adaptation plans enough layers and stops to give each child a turn. These are fairness choices, not required properties of every version.

## Variants

- **Layer rewards or actions:** Layers may contain a treat or task rather than only wrapping. This changes what the holder does at step 3, not the pass/stop/unwrapping loop. [United Reformed Church](https://urc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Holiday-club-book-Clubhouse-2025-online.pdf)
- **Everyone gets a turn:** Prepare enough layers and guide stops so every child unwraps once. This changes the host's stop policy. [National Day Nurseries Association](https://ndna.org.uk/blog/christmas-party-games/)
- **Discussion prompts:** Put numbered questions under the layers. After unwrapping, the holder answers and the group briefly discusses before play resumes. The cited version also redirects repeat turns to someone who has not answered. [Momentum Norfolk activity kit](https://www.momentumnorfolk.org.uk/assets/Uploads/Activity-Kit-Full-Document.pdf)

## Exceptions and open questions

- **Documented recovery rules:** The Momentum Norfolk discussion version passes a repeat turn onward to an unanswered participant. Other cited versions do not state a recovery rule for repeat turns.
- **Unspecified cases:** What happens if someone leaves mid-game, refuses to unwrap or perform a task, drops the parcel during a stop, or if the music fails? The sources do not settle these cases. The host must also decide whether the number of layers should match the number of participants.
- **Age and materials:** The early-years source calls for age-appropriate materials and supervision; its caution includes allergies and choking. These affect preparation, not the core algorithm. [National Day Nurseries Association](https://ndna.org.uk/blog/christmas-party-games/)

## Mechanisms

Passing token; circular turn order; host-controlled start/stop trigger; one-layer consumption; concealed contents; terminal reveal; optional turn-balancing; optional prompt-and-discussion after each stop.

## Access and participation

- **Demands created by the rules:** Participants need to perceive the start/stop signal, locate and pass the parcel, and remove a layer when selected. A discussion-prompt variant also asks for spoken participation.
- **Source-backed adaptations:** [RNIB's party guide](https://www.rnib.org.uk/documents/465/APDF-RE190403_Parties_and_Playdates-v07.pdf) says to explain each stage, announce starts and finishes, use precise spatial directions, and allow peer help with unwrapping for children with vision impairment. It identifies the game's sound and tactile cues as useful in this context.
- **Participant and host choices:** Following [Playworks' access guidance](https://www.playworks.org/resource/tips-for-making-play-access-easier/), the host can ask what help is wanted and explain who is passing the parcel to whom. The sources do not prescribe a universal approach to declining a turn or a prompt.
- **Unverified access questions:** Alternative cues if the music cannot be heard; what support is appropriate for a particular participant; how to keep a turn meaningful if another person assists with unwrapping.

## Possible software role (analysis, not a source rule)

- **No software needed:** A host, music, and a physical parcel suffice.
- **Optional host assistance:** A cue sheet, music control, layer count, or simple record of who has unwrapped.
- **Participant-facing or automated functions, if justified:** None are necessary for the cited physical versions.
- **Physical or human state that software cannot determine on its own:** Who actually holds the parcel, whether a layer was removed, and whether a child needs help or wishes to decline.
