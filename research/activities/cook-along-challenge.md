# Cook Along Challenge

## Identity and context

- **Description:** Distant participants prepare the same recipe and share the experience either live or by a deadline.
- **Group and mode:** The Family Dinner Project says two or more; remote, live or asynchronous.
- **Facilitator:** An organizer distributes the recipe and coordinates a time.
- **Experience:** Cooking together across distance and comparing results.

## Preparation

Participants agree on a new or familiar recipe. The organizer sends recipe and instructions, and the group chooses either a live meeting time or a submission deadline. Each participant obtains ingredients and supplies.

## Procedure

1. Agree on recipe and branch: live or separately.
2. **Live:** At the chosen time, join a group call, cook the dish together, exchange help and encouragement, and show or taste results on camera.
3. **Separate:** Cook and taste independently; take photos or video and record reactions. Share these with the group by the agreed deadline.
4. The challenge ends after the live session or after results are shared by the deadline. The source does not prescribe judging or winners.

## Rules and choices

- **Shared input:** One chosen recipe and instructions.
- **Timing:** Meeting time for live cooking; deadline for separate cooking.
- **Visibility:** Live cooking and results are visible on the call; separate results are shared after participants complete them.
- **Host choice:** Recipe, communication service, time, and mode are group decisions.

## Variants

Live and separate cooking are two explicit branches in the [source](https://thefamilydinnerproject.org/fun_content/cook-along-challenge/), not an inferred conversion. One participant may teach a family recipe.

## Exceptions and open questions

The source does not specify late results, unavailable ingredients, a participant unable to join the call, or whether everyone must complete the recipe before the group finishes.

## Access and participation

The activity requires ingredients, kitchen access, and a way to share the experience. The live branch needs a group connection and may use camera; the separate branch needs a way to send photos or video. The source gives no accessibility or food-allergy protocol. Recipe and equipment choices need participant-specific planning, and cooking safety remains a human responsibility.

## Mechanisms and possible software role

**Mechanisms:** Shared artifact specification, branch between synchronized and deadline-based participation, media sharing. Software could distribute instructions, schedule, and collect results. It cannot verify safe preparation or tasting without people reporting them.
