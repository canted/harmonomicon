# Day One Shared Journal

## Identity and context

Day One's Shared Journal is an invitation-only space where members add entries and respond to one another. The [current product guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/shared-journals/shared-journals/) separates personal and shared journals. Day One also has [daily writing prompts](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/tips-and-tutorials/daily-writing-prompts/), but the sources consulted do not prescribe a shared daily challenge combining the two features.

## Documented procedure

1. Create a new Shared Journal, then generate an invitation. The owner approves or declines join requests.
2. An approved member adds a dated entry to the Shared Journal. The journal timeline shows its author, media preview, reactions, and comment count.
3. Other members read, comment on, or react to the entry. Members may continue adding entries without a fixed end.

## Rules and choices

- A personal journal cannot simply be converted into a shared one; an entry can be copied or moved with a confirmation step. This is an explicit privacy boundary.
- Access is limited to invited members. The current guide says there is no public web publishing of shared entries.
- The owner can choose author-only editing or allow members to edit one another's entries on supported platforms. When collaborative editing is enabled, one person edits an entry at a time.
- The sources do not require a daily entry, a common prompt, or a completion mark. Those would be **additional activity rules**, not built-in properties of Shared Journals.

## Mechanisms and software boundary

**Mechanisms:** invitation and approval, persistent shared artifact collection, author attribution, comments/reactions, private-to-shared transition, configurable edit rights. This case makes **content visibility and edit permission** central even without a clock or completion deadline.
