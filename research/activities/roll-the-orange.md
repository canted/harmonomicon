# Roll the Orange

## Identity and context

- **Description:** A physical orange or ball marks who may speak at a dinner table.
- **Group and mode:** Family or table group; in person, live. The source gives no fixed size.
- **Facilitator:** A person introduces the rule and can check whether everyone has had a turn.
- **Experience:** More orderly, inclusive conversation.

## Preparation

Place an orange or small ball at the table. For the structured version, provide a conversation-starter jar or device.

## Procedure

1. Choose a first person. They read a conversation starter, take the orange, then roll it to another person.
2. The recipient answers while holding the orange. Others wait rather than answer.
3. When finished, that person rolls it to someone else. Repeat until everyone has answered.

## Rules and choices

- **Roles/assignment:** The holder has the speaking turn and chooses the next recipient.
- **Trigger:** A speaker finishes and passes the object; no timer is specified.
- **Visibility:** The current holder and spoken contributions are public to the table.
- **Completion:** Everyone has had a turn in the structured version. No end rule is given for open conversation.
- **Host choice:** The source suggests checking whether someone has been missed.

## Variants

In open conversation, omit the starter and let someone raise a hand or wave a napkin to request the orange. A reciprocal-conversation version sets a target number of rolls. Both are in the [source](https://thefamilydinnerproject.org/fun_content/roll-the-orange/).

## Exceptions and open questions

The source does not specify what happens when the chosen recipient declines, interrupts, cannot handle the object, or wants to ask a clarification without the token. The host must settle those locally.

## Access and participation

The physical pass and spoken answer require movement, vision or other awareness of the current holder, and conversational participation. The source permits a small ball instead of an orange and provides a gesture to request a turn, but gives no broader adaptation. A host should ask participants what assistance works, following [Playworks' access guidance](https://www.playworks.org/resource/tips-for-making-play-access-easier/). Passing on a question is an open rule, not a documented variant.

## Mechanisms and possible software role

**Mechanisms:** Possession grants a speaking right; chosen-recipient handoff; coverage check. A software host could display a prompt or track turns, but the physical holder and actual speech require human observation.
