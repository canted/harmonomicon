# Gratitude Spies

## Identity and context

- **Description:** Children receive a secret peer assignment, notice something appreciative during the day, and share it in a closing circle.
- **Group and mode:** Classroom, in person; observation is spread across a day and sharing is live. No size is specified.
- **Facilitator:** A teacher prepares a name-drawing method and convenes the closing circle.
- **Experience:** Directed attention and acknowledgement among peers.

## Preparation

Put classmates' names in a hat or equivalent draw pool. Explain that each child draws another student's name without revealing it.

## Procedure

1. At the start of the day, each child privately draws a peer's name.
2. During the day, each child observes their assigned peer for something they appreciate.
3. At day's end, convene a circle. Each child shares one thing they are grateful for about the assigned peer.
4. The round ends after sharing; the source does not prescribe repetition across days.

## Rules and choices

- **Assignment:** Draw from a hat; the name remains secret until the sharing stage. The source does not say whether a self-draw is redrawn or whether each peer must be assigned exactly once.
- **Trigger:** Start-of-day draw and end-of-day circle.
- **Visibility:** Assignment is private; appreciation is shared with the circle.
- **Completion:** End-of-day sharing, with no stated rule for missed turns.

## Variants

No variant of this particular activity is specified in the cited source.

## Exceptions and open questions

Self-draws, duplicate assignments, absences, no observation to share, and a child wishing not to speak are unresolved. A software version must not invent a one-to-one assignment rule from the word “draw.”

## Access and participation

The activity requires access to the assignment, observation during the day, and later expression in a group. It may involve personal disclosure about another child. The source provides no adaptations or consent rule; any such rule would be a host choice and must be explicit in a portable package.

## Mechanisms and possible software role

**Mechanisms:** Private random assignment, delayed real-world observation, scheduled group reveal. Software could deliver private assignments or reminders; it cannot verify whether genuine observation occurred. The cited activity does not require storing comments.
