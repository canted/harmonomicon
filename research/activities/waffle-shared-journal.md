# Waffle shared journal

## Identity and context

Waffle is a shared journal for a chosen group such as partners, family, or friends. Members can write and read in the same journal; daily prompts are offered as a way to start conversation. This is an **ongoing shared space**, not necessarily a challenge with a start date or a submission deadline.

## Documented procedure

1. Create a journal and include the people who will share it.
2. Members may answer a daily prompt or write freely. They can add entries and supported media; other members can read and comment. The FAQ also lists a read-state feature.
3. Members continue over time, using the journal as a shared record and a place for asynchronous response.

## Rules and gaps

- The product offers question decks and personalized prompts; its [journal ideas guide](https://www.wafflejournal.com/blog/waffle-journal-ideas) says the daily prompt may be used or skipped in favor of free writing.
- The consulted sources do not impose one entry per person per day, an end date, a group completion rule, or a penalty for missed days.
- Waffle's FAQ says a journal owner cannot currently remove another person; members leave on their own. This differs from other shared-journal products and is an access/ownership rule, not an intrinsic property of group journaling.

## Mechanisms and software boundary

**Mechanisms:** bounded membership, persistent shared space, optional daily prompt, media entry, comments, read state, ongoing history. A protocol would need to distinguish prompt scheduling from permissions on the shared journal and entries. Whether an entry is a private draft before posting is not established by these sources.
